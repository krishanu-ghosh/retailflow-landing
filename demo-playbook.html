How to use this

Going down the sidebar top-to-bottom front-loads Dashboard and back-loads the modules that actually make a prospect say yes. This script is built around four acts instead, ordered by what a buyer cares about, not by where a screen sits in the nav or which table it reads from:

Act I

A day at the counter

Sell something, collect a payment, watch the cash and the ledger react. The complete money story, told fast.

Act II

Proof it adds up

Everything from Act I rolling up live into KPIs and a real drill-down. The payoff moment.

Act III

Running the back office

Catalog, stock counts, suppliers and purchasing — the setup and upkeep behind Acts I and II.

Act IV

Trust & control

Roles, an audit trail, and the dials that make this business behave like itself, not a generic one.

The practical upshot: each length below is a fixed, curated set of stages — not simply "the first N acts" — so you always know exactly what's in and out before you're on the call.

≈ 35 min

Quick

POS Billing, Sales History, Customers, Dashboard, Products & Catalog, Suppliers & Purchases (Stages 1, 2, 3, 6, 8, 10). The full sell → prove-it → restock loop, without the Reports drill-down, cash/expense bookkeeping, or the trust-and-control stack.

Use when: a shorter call or a prospect pressed for time — it still closes the operational loop and backs it with real numbers on the Dashboard.

≈ 49 min

Standard

Everything in Quick, plus Reports, Expenses, Stock Count, and Settings (adds Stages 5, 7, 9, 13). Layers in the report drill-down, cash discipline, shrinkage/loss prevention, and the configuration story.

Use when: the default length for most calls with the actual decision-maker — past pure discovery, before the governance deep-dive.

≈ 58 min

Full

Everything in Standard, plus Cashbook, Audit Log, and Users & Roles (adds Stages 4, 11, 12) — all 13 stages.

Use when: a second call, a multi-stakeholder room, or anyone who'll push on roles, audit, or data control before they'll sign off.

Tap a length above to dim everything outside that path — tap it again to clear.

Before the call

  • Log in as an Owner-role user — the only role with all 22 permissions, so nothing is hidden mid-call. If you're deliberately demonstrating role differences, decide in advance which stage you'll switch to a Manager or Cashier login for.
  • Confirm none of the seven toggleable modules are hidden for this tenant — Suppliers, Purchases, Expenses, Cashbook, Stock Count, Audit Log, Categories. That list is managed outside the tenant app, so check with onboarding/support ahead of time rather than discovering a missing sidebar item live.
  • Seed 15–20 products across 2–3 categories, a few with photos, at least one with its own batch barcode, and one sitting below its minimum stock — you need that last one for the reorder/low-stock moment in Stages 6 and 8.
  • Seed 2–3 customers with phone numbers (for the WhatsApp invoice and credit demo) and 1–2 suppliers.
  • Backdate a handful of sales, purchases, and expenses so Dashboard and Reports aren't empty on first load — an empty chart undersells the analytics story badly.
  • If you're demoing the camera barcode scanner, use HTTPS (or localhost) and grant camera permission before the call starts — it refuses to run in an insecure context.
  • If you're demoing the UPI QR at checkout, confirm the tenant has a UPI VPA configured — it shows read-only in Settings, so set it up beforehand if missing.
  • Keep a phone on hand to actually receive the WhatsApp invoice — it's a real wa.me deep link; worth tapping once in rehearsal so you're not surprised live.

Act I · Stages 1–5

A day at the counter

Everything a shop owner emotionally cares about first: sell something, get paid, watch the cash and the ledger react. Told start to finish before touching a single setup screen.

Stage 01

POS Billing

≈ 6 min
/sales/new sales.create reports.profit (margin preview)

This is the screen a cashier lives in all day. Open here, not the dashboard — a real sale in the first sixty seconds is what makes everything after it feel earned rather than staged.

Finding a product

  • Type-ahead search — press F1 anywhere on the screen to jump straight to it, searches active products by name with price shown inline.
  • Dedicated barcode field for a USB/keyboard-wedge scanner: scan, it submits on Enter automatically.
  • No scanner handy? Any fast keystroke sequence ending in Enter is treated as a scan — works with any wedge scanner, no setup screen needed.
  • 📷 Scan opens the device camera — reads EAN-13/8, UPC-A/E, Code128/39/93, ITF, Codabar and QR, with a torch toggle and an aiming guide; falls back to a WASM scanner automatically on iOS Safari where the native API isn't available.
  • Empty search box shows a Recently sold quick-add grid (12 tiles, refreshed after every sale) — a good one-tap moment for a shop's bread-and-butter items.
  • Out-of-stock and deactivated products are blocked with a clear toast, not a silent failure or a broken cart.
  • If a product has stock split across more than one active batch, a batch picker appears — batch number, remaining qty, price, and (with margin permission on) per-unit profit.

Building the cart

  • Qty stepper or type a quantity directly — fractional quantities work for KG/LITER-priced items, capped at total stock with a "Max stock: N" hint.
  • Per-line discount, toggled between ₹ and %.
  • Bill-level discount (₹ or %) spreads proportionally across lines that don't already carry their own discount — nothing gets double-discounted.
  • Optional warranty checkbox with a month picker (1–600 months) and a live "valid up to" date preview.
  • Running totals: Subtotal, Discount, Tax (GST), Final price — plus, when margin permission is on, a live projected profit and margin %, colored red if negative. Good moment to show a Manager-level login sees this and a Cashier login doesn't.

Taking payment — F3 / F4

  • Customer picker: search by name or phone, "Walk-in" for no account, or add a brand-new customer inline without leaving the sale.
  • If the customer already carries store credit, a banner offers to apply it in one click.
  • Methods: Cash, UPI, Card, Bank Transfer, or Credit against the customer's own balance — plus "Exact cash" and "Full credit" one-tap shortcuts.
  • "Split across multiple methods" reveals all four amount fields at once and keeps them balanced as you type.
  • "Generate QR" renders a genuine UPI deep-link QR code using the business's configured VPA.
  • Change owed can be handed back as cash, or — one checkbox — credited straight to the customer's account instead.
  • "Print Invoice" is pre-checked from a business setting; completing the sale streams a real PDF into a new tab and triggers print.
  • The Complete button's label updates live to say exactly what will happen — "Complete — ₹40 credit," "Complete — Change ₹15" — worth calling out as a small trust-building detail.
Why it lands well

Nothing here is trusted from the browser — totals, batch allocation, and payment validation are all recomputed server-side. A cashier physically cannot push a discount past what a manager should see.

Know this gap

There's no hold/park-sale today — a cart is either completed or abandoned, there's no suspend-and-resume for a second customer mid-transaction. If asked, say so plainly rather than improvising around it.

Ring up this sale as a credit sale for an existing customer, paid partly in cash — you'll need both threads in the next three stages. Then walk straight into Sales.

Stage 02

Sales History

≈ 4 min
/sales sales.view sales.cancel sales.refund

Show the sale you just rang up landing here instantly, then use that exact sale to demo void, refund, and the WhatsApp send — all from one drawer.

The list

  • Columns: Invoice #, Customer, Date, Total, Paid, Balance (color-coded), Status badge (Active / Voided / Refunded).
  • Search by invoice number; a day-or-range date filter with a one-click "Clear dates."
  • Two distinct empty states — genuinely no sales, versus filters excluding everything (with its own "clear filters" link) — small detail, but it keeps a filtered demo from looking broken.
  • Row actions: Details drawer, and — only when the sale has a customer with a phone on file — a one-tap Send on WhatsApp that opens a prefilled, itemized invoice message.

The detail drawer

  • Full line items, including batch allocation when a sale spanned batches (e.g. "BATCH123 ×2, BATCH124 ×1").
  • Payments list, warranty block if applicable, and Print Invoice.
  • Record payment — for a credit sale with a balance, reuses the same oldest-invoice-first allocation logic shown next, in Customers.
  • Void — auto-disables once the business's configured void window (default 12 hours) has passed, with a tooltip explaining why.
  • Refund — auto-disables once the refund window (default 7 days) has passed; choose to refund to the customer's credit balance or pay cash straight from the register.
Know this gap

No bulk actions, no column sort, and no CSV export on this screen — bulk export only lives in Reports (Stage 7).

That sale left a balance on a real customer's account — jump to their record and show it already reflected, no refresh needed.

Stage 03

Customers

≈ 5 min
/customers customers.manage sales.view / sales.create

This is where the credit sale from Stage 1 pays off — pull up that exact customer and show their balance already reflecting it.

The list & detail

  • List: search, an Outstanding column color-coded (red = owes you, amber "CR" = you owe them, green = settled), status badge, quick actions.
  • Detail is tabbed: Customer Details (code, phone, email, GSTIN, address, notes), Statement (full running-balance ledger of every payment/refund/adjustment), Invoices (all sales, "unpaid only" filter, WhatsApp send per invoice), Products Bought (month or all-time, with purchase counts and last-purchased date).

Collecting a payment

  • Auto-allocates the amount received across open invoices oldest-first, with per-invoice checkboxes to override manually.
  • "Clear all dues" one-click prefill of the full amount owed.
  • If the customer already carries credit, a one-click "Apply ₹X credit" button appears with the exact safely-applicable amount already computed.
  • On full payoff, offers a printable "dues cleared" receipt.

Pay out credit

  • A separate, clearly distinct flow for handing money back to a customer sitting in credit (say, after a refund) — fully ledgered, not a workaround through a fake sale.
Know this gap

No credit-limit field and no loyalty/points program. Balances are always derived live from the ledger — never a stored number, so they can't silently drift — but there's no hard credit cap enforced today. No CSV export of the customer list either.

Collect a small cash payment against that balance right now — some of the money from this stage, plus the cash portion of Stage 1's sale, has to land somewhere. Show exactly where.

Stage 04

Cashbook

≈ 4 min
/cashbook payments.manage settings.manage (opening balance) cashbook

The cash portion of Stage 1's sale, and the payment you just collected in Stage 3, are already sitting here — before you've touched Purchases or Expenses at all. This is the "does the drawer actually match" screen.

What's already here, and what will be

  • Header stats: Opening, Cash in, Cash out, Closing balance — always computed from the entry log, never a cached figure.
  • Entries tab — a fully cross-referenced feed; every row shows exactly which sale, customer payment, purchase, expense, or refund generated it. Every entry so far traces back to Stage 1 or Stage 3 — later, once Expenses and Purchasing are wired up, cash movements from those land here the same way.
  • Report tab — a Day / Month / Range / All-time filter giving a true running daily opening-to-closing ledger, latest-first. This filter set is a very recent addition, so it's a fair, current talking point that the product is actively improving.
  • Manual tools: + Adjustment (cash in/out with a note, for anything not sale/purchase/expense-driven) and, for anyone with settings access, Set opening balance.
Why it lands well

Entries are append-only — corrections (an edited expense, a purchase price variance) are always a new offsetting entry, never a rewrite of history. Nothing here can quietly disappear.

Cash coming in from sales and collections is covered — the other side of the same coin is what goes out the door.

Stage 05

Expenses

≈ 3 min
/expenses expenses.manage expenses

Quick stage, but it's what turns "gross profit" into "net profit" two stages from now on the Dashboard.

  • Expenses tab — date range, category, and payment-method filters; one summary card (total + entry count); inline edit/delete per row.
  • New expense: category (inline-create), date, amount, payment method, optional vendor, optional note.
  • Categories tab — name/description, deactivate/reactivate (soft, reversible).
  • Report tab — month or all-time filter, group by Category, Vendor, or Payment method, with a simple horizontal-bar breakdown.
Why it lands well

Record a cash expense here, then flip straight back to Cashbook — the matching entry is already there. No separate double-entry step for the cashier or bookkeeper to remember.

Know this gap

No approval workflow and no recurring expenses today — every entry is recorded and immediately final; recurring rent/salary still has to be re-entered each period.

Sales, customer payments, cash, expenses — that's the whole money story for this business. Now show it roll up into one screen that updates itself.

Act II · Stages 6–7

Proof it adds up

Everything just entered across Act I, live in one KPI screen and one drill-down. This is the payoff — the moment the demo stops being a feature tour and starts looking like a decision.

Stage 06

Dashboard

≈ 5 min
/dashboard reports.profit reports.sales / reports.inventory (widgets)

Refreshes on its own every 30 seconds. Everything you rang up in Act I is already moving these numbers — if there's a natural pause, ring up one more small sale right now and let a figure change on screen without touching refresh.

  • Hero row: Today's Sales (+ order count), Today's Profit (red/green by sign), Monthly Revenue (with a growth-vs-prior-period badge), Monthly Profit.
  • Inventory alerts: Reorder-soon list (with a suggested order quantity) and Low-stock list — both promoted above the fold automatically whenever there's something to act on, otherwise tucked below so they're never fully hidden. You'll set up the minimum-stock threshold behind these firsthand two stages from now, in Products.
  • Sales trend: 30 / 90 / 365-day toggle, a dual revenue + gross-profit area chart with a per-day tooltip, collapsible.
  • Category chart: revenue + gross profit by category, top 8, collapsible — collapsing it makes room for a Top Customers side card.
  • Financial position row: Inventory value, Cash position, Receivable, Payable.
Why it lands well

This screen is deliberately read-only — every number here has a matching drill-down one click away in Reports, which is exactly the next stop.

Every number just shown has a one-click-deeper version — go find it.

Stage 07

Reports

≈ 6 min
/reports reports.sales reports.inventory / reports.profit (per tab)

Six tabs, each answering a different question a different stakeholder in the room actually has.

  • Overview — revenue / orders / AOV / growth; gross profit / expenses / net profit / margin; category chart. Month or all-time.
  • Inventory — valuation, low stock, reorder suggestions, dead stock (with a "last sale" date), full valuation table.
  • Rankings — top customers, top suppliers (supplier data gets a proper introduction two stages from now, in Suppliers & Purchases), ABC analysis (revenue-share classing), fast-moving products.
  • Sales — products sold by day, single-day or date-range, with unit and revenue totals.
  • Financial — revenue / COGS / gross profit / expenses / net profit, plus cash position and receivable/payable — clearly labeled as current-only when viewing a past month.
  • Exports — one-click CSV or Excel for 10 report types: low stock, reorder, dead stock, fast-moving, slow-moving, valuation, ABC, category performance, top customers, top suppliers.
Why it lands well

Every export is itself audit-logged — who exported what, and when. A nice governance detail for a compliance-minded buyer, and a preview of Act IV.

You've now seen every rupee this business will ever move and every KPI it will ever watch — none of it means anything without a catalog behind it. Time to build one.

Act III · Stages 8–10

Running the back office

The setup and day-to-day upkeep that made Acts I and II possible in the first place — catalog, physical stock, and where it all comes from. Less flashy, so it comes after the hook and the payoff, not before them.

Stage 08

Products & Catalog

≈ 9 min
/products products.view / create / update / delete inventory.adjust categories

The densest screen in the app, and the one that convinces an owner the catalog will actually match their real shop — variable units, per-batch cost tracking, and a barcode-first workflow throughout.

Adding / editing a product

  • Photo: gallery picker or a forced-camera capture, auto-compressed above 2MB, uploaded straight to cloud storage.
  • Name, SKU, Category (with inline "+ Add category"), Purchase price, Selling price.
  • GST toggle with slab picker — 5 / 12 / 18 / 28 / 40% — and a "Selling price is MRP" checkbox for tax-inclusive pricing.
  • Unit: PCS, KG, GRAM, LITER, ML, BOX, PACK, DOZEN, METER.
  • Min. stock — this is the threshold behind the reorder/low-stock cards you already saw on the Dashboard. Barcode (type or camera-scan), Initial stock (create only).
  • On edit, purchase price becomes a read-only "latest batch cost" — cost now lives at the batch level. That's the cue for the next section.

Batches — the lot-costing story

  • Every product's stock lives in batches — B-1, B-2, … — each carrying its own cost, sell price, barcode, and source (opening stock or a specific purchase).
  • Editing a batch's cost or price doesn't rewrite history — a sale already made against that batch keeps its original cost for margin reporting.
  • Deactivate / reactivate a single batch to pull it from the till without touching quantity or history.
  • A cross-product All batches screen — search and filter across every batch in the shop, same edit actions in one place.
  • New stock always lands in a fresh batch at current cost; stock removed (adjustment or count) draws down oldest-batch-first — genuine FIFO, not a single running number.

Categories

  • Name + description, a live product count per category, deactivate/reactivate (soft, reversible) — and can be created inline from the product form itself.

The product list

  • Search, category filter, an A–Z jump bar that also groups the current page into collapsible letter sections.
  • Low-stock rows render in bold red the moment stock falls to or below the product's minimum.
  • An "(MRP)" tag appears next to selling price when tax-inclusive pricing is on.
  • Per-row: Batches, Stock (adjust), Edit, Deactivate/Reactivate — deactivating is a reversible soft-delete, never destructive.

Stock adjustment

  • A single signed delta ("+add / −remove") with a live "new stock will be" preview and a free-text reason note — posts through the same batch/FIFO mechanics as everything else in the app.
Know this gap

No multi-select/bulk actions and no CSV import or export on the product list — worth flagging up front if the prospect has a large existing catalog they'll want to migrate in bulk.

Catalog's covered — now show what happens when the shelf and the system disagree.

Stage 09

Stock Count

≈ 3 min
/inventory-audits inventory.audit inventory_audits

The "trust but verify" feature — a physical count that automatically corrects system stock and tells the owner exactly what they lost or found, in rupees.

Stock counts tab

  • New count: pick a date, add products (auto-fills current system stock as the starting point), type the physical count, watch a live color-coded difference column, remove rows before saving.
  • Detail view: count of short lines, count of over lines, one net rupee-value summary, and the full item table.
  • Complete count & correct stock — a single, irreversible action that reconciles every line (new batch for surplus, FIFO draw-down for shortage) and flips the count to Completed.

Loss report tab ("Shrinkage")

  • Month or date-range filter, one headline total loss/surplus figure, and the same product-level table aggregated across every completed count in that window.
Know this gap

Once completed, a count can't be reopened — stage a deliberate demo count rather than clicking "Complete" on a real accidental one live.

Stock is airtight — now show where it actually comes from.

Stage 10

Suppliers & Purchases

≈ 6 min
/suppliers, /purchases suppliers.manage purchases.view / create / update suppliers purchases

This closes the operational loop — show a purchase order arrive and turn straight into the sellable batches you saw built by hand in Stage 8.

Suppliers

  • Same shape as Customers: code, contact person, GSTIN, address, notes, status — plus a Purchases tab and a Products Delivered tab on the detail page.
  • Statement drawer shows what's owed (or a supplier credit balance), and a "record refund received" flow for money coming back from a supplier.

Creating a purchase order

  • Pick or inline-create a supplier, optional supplier invoice #, add line items (pick or inline-create a product), per-line qty/cost/tax, header discount, live subtotal/tax/total.
  • Saved as Draft — posts the liability to the supplier ledger immediately, no stock movement yet.

Receiving goods — the batch-creation moment

  • Per line: quantity received now, unit cost (flags a live variance if it differs from the order), selling price, and a batch barcode (scan, or reuse the previous one).
  • Each delivery becomes its own new batch — or appends to an existing one from the same PO if the cost matches — the direct payoff of the Batches story from Stage 8.
  • Partial deliveries track line-by-line; status moves Draft → Partially received → Received automatically.

Other purchase actions

  • Record payment — cash payments post straight to the Cashbook you saw back in Stage 4.
  • Return items — per-line return qty + reason, credits the supplier.
  • Close short — writes off quantity that will never arrive.
  • List: search by PO number, date filter; a purchase's header shows Total, Outstanding, Returned, Price variance, Written off.

Sell, count, restock — the full operational loop is closed. Last thing left to prove: none of it can be quietly abused by staff.

Act IV · Stages 11–13

Trust & control

Who can do what, who did what, and the handful of dials that make this business behave like itself. Closes the demo on confidence rather than a feature list.

Stage 11

Users & Roles

≈ 3 min
/users users.manage

Three fixed roles — Owner, Manager, Cashier — and every sensitive action asks the acting admin to re-enter their own password before it goes through. Say that second part out loud; it isn't the default in most systems this size.

  • New user: name, email, password (set directly — no email-invite step), phone, role. Seat count is capped and shown live as "N / limit accounts," enforced server-side, not just hidden in the UI.
  • Edit user, Reset password (someone else's), Change role, Deactivate — every one of these requires the acting admin to re-confirm their own password, and none of them can target your own account (self-service password change lives in Settings instead).
  • 22 granular permissions sit underneath — sales, products, inventory, purchasing, suppliers, customers, payments, expenses, three flavors of reports, users, settings, and audit — bundled today into the three roles above.
Know this gap

No custom role builder yet. If a prospect wants a role sitting between Manager and Cashier, be upfront that it isn't configurable today — the 22 permissions exist, but only as three fixed bundles.

Last stop — where every one of those actions gets a permanent paper trail.

Stage 12

Audit Log

≈ 2 min
/audit-logs audit_logs.view audit_log

Point at literally anything done earlier in the call and show it landed here — timestamped, with the acting user, their role at the time, and their IP address.

  • Filter: date range (no user or action-type filter in the UI yet).
  • Columns: Time, Action (color-coded), Entity, Entity ID, User, Role, IP address.
Know this gap

Nearly 60 distinct action types are captured across every module shown today, plus full before/after value snapshots stored on disk — but those snapshots aren't surfaced in the UI yet. Don't promise a live diff view; it's fair to say the data already exists and a viewer is on the roadmap.

Last thing — the handful of dials that make this app behave like this business, not a generic one.

Stage 13

Settings

≈ 2 min
/settings settings.manage reports.inventory

Deliberately short — most of what makes this business look and feel like itself is set once at onboarding, not fiddled with day to day.

  • Business details — read-only display (name, phone, GSTIN, address, UPI ID/name); changes go through support, keeping the business's legal/financial identity stable.
  • Inventory & sales settings (editable here) — lead time, safety-stock days, dead-stock days, low-stock threshold, void window (days + hours), refund window (days), and "print invoice by default."
  • Change password.
Know this gap

No invoice-number customization (auto INV-YYYY-NNNNNN), no receipt template editor, no multi-currency (₹ / en-IN fixed), no timezone setting (Asia/Kolkata fixed), no notification/alert settings. If any of these is a hard requirement, surface it before the call ends rather than let it be a surprise later.

Objection handling

Every gap called out inline above, in one place for a five-minute pre-call review. Nothing here is a weakness to hide — each one has a straight, honest answer.

No hold / park sale
A cart is completed or abandoned today — no suspend-and-resume mid-transaction.
No loyalty / points program
Credit and ledger tracking exist; a points/rewards layer doesn't, today.
No hard credit-limit enforcement
Balances are always live from the ledger — accurate, just not capped.
No custom roles
22 permissions exist under the hood, bundled into three fixed roles only.
No bulk actions / CSV import
Sales, Products, Customers and Suppliers lists are single-row actions only; bulk export lives solely in Reports.
No recurring expenses or approval workflow
Every expense entry is final and manually re-entered each period.
No invoice/receipt customization
Invoice numbering is auto-sequenced; no template/branding editor yet.
No multi-currency or timezone setting
Fixed to ₹ / en-IN / Asia-Kolkata today.
Audit log has no drill-down
Before/after values are captured and stored, just not rendered in the UI yet.
Module visibility isn't tenant-editable
The seven toggleable modules are hidden/shown by RetailFlow, not by the business itself — frame this as "contact support," never demo it live.
